PRD — Netflix: Recommended by Friends
1. Product Overview
Feature: Recommended by Friends
Platform: Netflix
Target segment: Indian Netflix users aged 60–80
Initial scope: Mobile & Tablet
Product Idea
Focus on social recommendations by improving the SHARE experience.
The goal is to make it easier for users to discover content through recommendations from people they know and trust, without requiring them to switch between Netflix and external communication platforms.
________________


2. Motivation
Senior citizens can have strong social circles and frequently discuss movies, TV shows, and other content with friends and family.
Today, recommendations can happen across multiple platforms.
For example:
Neha watches a movie and shares it with Yogesh on WhatsApp. Yogesh will only discover the recommendation when he opens WhatsApp, finds the message, and clicks the Netflix link.
This creates a fragmented discovery journey:
Friend recommends → External platform → User discovers message → Clicks link → Netflix → Starts watching
The recommendation is therefore dependent on the user accessing another platform.
Opportunity
What if Netflix could surface recommendations from friends inside Netflix itself?
When Yogesh opens Netflix, he could see:
Recommended by Friends
This creates a seamless journey:
Friend recommends → Netflix → Recommended by Friends → Watch
The objective isn't to turn Netflix into a social network. The objective is to make trusted social recommendations an integrated part of content discovery.
________________


3. Problem Statement
Netflix provides algorithmic recommendations, but users may also rely on recommendations from people they personally know.
Currently, these recommendations are fragmented across WhatsApp, SMS, phone calls, and other channels.
This can lead to:
* Recommendations being missed
* Users switching between apps
* Additional effort to search for the recommended title
* Increased decision-making time
* Lost opportunities for content discovery
Core Problem
Users need a simple way to discover and act on content recommended by friends and family without having to search across different platforms.
________________

5. User Hypothesis
If users can easily share Netflix content with friends and see recommendations from friends directly inside Netflix, they will discover relevant content faster and be more likely to start watching it.

7. Proposed Solution
A. Share with Friends
Enhance the existing Share experience.
When a user is watching or viewing the details of a title:
Share → Share with Friends → Select Friend/Group → Send
The user can optionally add a message.
Example:
Neha recommended
The Intern
“You might enjoy this!”
Share placement
The feature should also be easily discoverable near existing engagement actions such as:
👍 Thumbs Up | 👎 Thumbs Down | Share with Friends
This increases visibility without introducing a completely new interaction pattern.
________________


8. Recommended by Friends
Introduce a new section on the Netflix Home page:
Recommended by Friends
This section appears when the user has received at least one recommendation.
Each recommendation could display:
The Intern
Recommended by Neha
▶ Watch
If multiple recommendations exist, users can browse them in the same familiar Netflix content-card format.
Important principle
The section should be contextual rather than permanently occupying homepage space.
If there are no recommendations, the section does not need to appear.
________________


User Context / Design Considerations
1. Primary viewing device — TV
Senior citizens are likely to consume Netflix primarily on TV, with a smaller proportion using mobile or laptop devices. Therefore, the sharing experience needs to work particularly well with a TV remote, where navigating through multiple menus can create significant friction.
Rather than requiring users to navigate:
Title → More Options → Share → Share with Friends → Select Group
the recommended interaction is:
👍 Thumbs Up | 👎 Thumbs Down | 👥 Share with Friends
With one click on “Share with Friends”, the user should only need to select the relevant group.
Design principle: Minimize navigation steps because every additional interaction on a TV remote increases friction.
________________
2. Simple, low-cognitive-load UX
The experience should be designed specifically for the needs of older users, including users who may have visual limitations, lower technology familiarity, or difficulty adapting to complex interfaces.
The UI should therefore prioritize:
* Large, clearly identifiable controls
* High readability and strong visual hierarchy
* Minimal number of steps
* Simple and familiar terminology
* Large touch/remote-selection targets
* Clear confirmation after an action
* Avoiding unnecessary pop-ups, menus and choices
* Consistent placement of the Share action
* Zero/near-zero cognitive load for the core journey
The ideal journey should be:
Click Share with Friends → Select Group → Done
rather than requiring the user to understand a new social workflow.
Design Principle
For senior users, simplicity is not a nice-to-have; it is a core product requirement.
9. Friends / Groups
Users can create a Friends/Family group for sharing recommendations.
Example:
Family Group
Members:
* Neha
* Yogesh
* Rajini
* Simran
When sharing:
Share with Friends → Select Group → Send
If the user belongs to multiple groups:
Select Group ▼
This allows recommendations to remain relevant to different social circles.
Examples:
* Family
* College Friends
* Neighbourhood Friends
* Travel Friends
________________


10. Privacy
Social viewing should always be opt-in.
Watching a title should not automatically notify friends.
When a user starts watching a title, Netflix can provide an optional action:
Want to share this with your friends?
If selected:
Select Group ▼ → Share
This can also be positioned alongside:
👍 | 👎 | Share with Friends
Privacy principles
* Sharing is explicit
* Watching does not automatically mean sharing
* User chooses the recipient/group
* Users can leave groups
* Users can mute recommendations
* No public viewing activity
________________


11. Future Opportunity
Once the recommendation-sharing behaviour is established, Netflix could introduce:
What Your Friends Are Watching
This could show content currently being watched by members of a user's friend/family group.
However, this should be treated as a future feature, not part of the MVP.
Why?
There is an important distinction:
Recommended by Friends
= Explicit user action
vs.
What Friends Are Watching
= Passive viewing activity
The second feature introduces significantly greater privacy considerations.
Therefore, it should only be introduced with explicit user consent.
For example:
Let your friends know you're watching this?
Yes / No

13. MVP
The MVP will focus only on the core social recommendation loop:
MVP
1. Share with Friends
2. Friend/Family Groups
3. Recommended by Friends section
4. Explicit privacy controls
Not part of MVP
* What Friends Are Watching
* Public profiles
* Comments
* Social feed
* Likes between friends
* Automatic viewing-status sharing
* Entire Netflix UI redesign
* New content categories
________________

15. Experimentation
Run an A/B test.
Control
Existing Netflix experience.
Treatment
Existing Netflix experience + Share with Friends + Recommended by Friends
Compare:
Content discovery → Recommendation engagement → Content start → Watch behaviour
The key question to validate:
Does adding trusted social recommendations help users find and start content more effectively?
________________


16. Product Success Definition
The feature will be considered successful if it demonstrates that trusted recommendations reduce discovery friction and increase content starts, while maintaining user privacy and not negatively affecting the existing Netflix experience.
________________


Core Product Bet
Netflix doesn't need to become a social network. It needs to make trusted recommendations easier to discover and act on.
